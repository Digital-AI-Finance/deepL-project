A content factory that shows its work

A working prototype of an AI content pipeline for a regulated fiduciary firm, built with no access to any client system. Everything here was produced by the pipeline and published through it.

The firm is invented. Northwind Fiduciary AG does not exist. Its profile, its regulatory perimeter and its approved-claim library are synthetic, and every asset on this site was written by an AI agent grounded on them.

What the pipeline does

A brief goes in. A writing agent drafts against a brand voice contract, a compliance rulebook and a library of pre-approved claims, and must declare every factual assertion it makes, each one resolving to a library entry or explicitly marked unsourced. Rule-based gates run. A risk-tier classifier decides who reviews it. A human approves it, or it does not publish. Every transition is written to a hash-chained ledger.

The rule that is not negotiable

Nothing publishes without a recorded human approval. That is enforced in four independent places, not one:

  1. the state machine has no route to published that avoids approved
  2. the router returns at least one reviewer for every risk tier
  3. the pipeline asserts an approval immediately before publishing
  4. the publishing channel refuses an unapproved asset outright

The fourth is the one that matters most: it is the only one a script bypassing the pipeline would still hit.

Read this before the content

Every asset on this site shows stand-in approvals recorded as AUTOMATED-NOT-A-HUMAN. This is a prototype with no human reviewers, and rather than invent plausible names the pipeline records what actually happened. The approvals table on each asset page says so.
