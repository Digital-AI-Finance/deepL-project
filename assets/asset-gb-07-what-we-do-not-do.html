The Limits of a Fiduciary's Role Before a Sale

blog · 1071 words · published 2026-08-17 · risk tier T3

Most descriptions of firms like ours are lists of what we can do. This one is about the edge of that list: what we do not do, who does it instead, and why the line sits where it does. If a sale of the business you built is somewhere between one and three years away, the boundary matters more to you than the capability list, because everything on the far side of it is work you will need someone else to carry.

The line, stated plainly

The firm does not provide investment advice or discretionary portfolio management. The firm does not provide tax advice and does not structure arrangements by reference to their tax outcome. The group does not take deposits, does not broker insurance, and does not carry on regulated activity in the United States or the United Kingdom.

What remains is narrower than the brochure version and more specific: holding and administering assets inside a legal structure, acting as professional trustee or serving on a foundation council, and doing the ongoing administration those roles require year after year. That is the whole of it.

Why the line is there and not somewhere else

Two reasons, and neither is modesty.

The first is authorisation. Northwind Fiduciary AG holds a trustee licence in Liechtenstein and is supervised by the FMA. A licence permits specified activities and does not permit others; a firm that drifts past its own authorisation is a supervisory problem before it is a commercial one. So the perimeter is written down rather than felt out case by case.

The second reason is that the roles conflict. A trustee owes duties to beneficiaries: to hold assets under the terms of the deed, to account, to act even when a family member is unhappy about it. A firm that also earns from selling the beneficiaries an investment product has bought itself an argument it can never fully answer. Keeping the trustee role clean is worth more to you than the convenience of one invoice.

What this means when the sale is eighteen months out

Owners usually arrive with the question "trust or foundation?" It is the wrong first question, and the answer to it changes very little.

The outline is short. A professional trustee holds and administers trust assets for the beneficiaries under the terms of the trust deed. A Liechtenstein foundation separates legal ownership of assets from the family that benefits from them, and a family foundation is established for a defined purpose set out in its founding documents, which govern how it may act. One is a relationship; the other is a legal person. Both can hold shares in your company. Neither is inherently the better choice.

The question that actually determines your outcome is one of sequence: what happens in which order, and which steps become expensive or impossible to reverse once taken. Transferring shares before the buyer's diligence starts is a different exercise from transferring them after heads of terms are signed. Succession structures generally take longer to put in place than the transactions that prompt them, so the work usually begins well before a sale. Choosing the vehicle is normally one of the last decisions, not the first.

Who does the parts we do not

Your corporate lawyer keeps the sale. Nothing about a holding or succession structure moves that work elsewhere, and a structure designed without the sale documents in front of it tends to need unpicking.

Tax belongs to a tax adviser, and if your accountant has not worked across borders before, this is the point at which they may want a specialist alongside them. That is a normal outcome, not a criticism of them.

Investment of the proceeds, once the proceeds exist, belongs to a bank or a licensed manager you appoint. We can hold the relationship inside the structure and see that the reporting arrives. We do not choose the manager for you and we do not sit behind the decision.

Residency, if the family moves, belongs to local counsel and to the authorities. A permit is granted by an authority, not by an adviser, and anyone who describes it otherwise is describing something else. Relocation support is coordinated with local counsel in the destination jurisdiction.

Coordination is a job, not a courtesy

The firm works alongside a client's existing tax and legal advisers rather than replacing them. In practice that means one agreed set of facts, one timeline that all of them are working from, and someone whose job it is to notice when the tax adviser's assumption and the sale agreement's drafting have quietly stopped matching. It is unglamorous and it is where most of the value sits.

It also means the administrative floor is visible from the start. The group operates under the automatic exchange of information and reports as required in each jurisdiction where it operates. Reporting is a fixed feature of any structure we would administer, and it is better priced into the decision at the beginning than discovered in year two.

When we are the wrong answer

If you, your family and the business all sit in one jurisdiction, the sale is a clean share purchase, and nobody is moving, a domestic will and a considered shareholders' agreement may do everything you need. Adding a cross-border structure would add cost and annual obligations for very little.

If what appeals to you about a structure is a tax result, we are the wrong firm; that is not what we sell and it is not what these arrangements are for.

And if a decision has to be final in six weeks, the honest answer is that the structure will not be properly in place by then. What can be done in six weeks is avoiding the steps that would close options later.

Where that leaves you

A reader one to three years from an exit is not yet choosing a vehicle. What is useful now is a map: the list of questions the sale will raise, marked with who owns each one. Writing that list down and asking whichever adviser you already trust most which items fall outside their own practice will tell you more than any comparison of trusts and foundations. Whatever falls off the edge of everyone's list is the part worth a conversation.
