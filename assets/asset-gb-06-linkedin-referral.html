What happens to your client relationship after you refer a matter to us

linkedin_post · 198 words · published 2026-08-17 · risk tier T3

A referral is a judgement call with your own name on it, so it is fair to ask what happens to the relationship afterwards.

You stay in it. We are brought in for the part that sits outside your practice area — usually a Liechtenstein foundation, or a trust holding assets in more than one jurisdiction — and we report back to you as well as to your client. The firm works alongside a client's existing tax and legal advisers rather than replacing them. That is not a courtesy. The structuring questions turn on domestic law points that you, not we, are qualified to answer, so the work only holds together if you remain part of it.

Two things we do not do, which may matter more than anything we do. The firm does not provide tax advice and does not structure arrangements by reference to their tax outcome. The firm does not provide investment advice or discretionary portfolio management. Those limits are deliberate, and they leave a good deal of your client's work where it already sits.

If a foundation or a trust is the wrong answer for your client, we will say so, and say why.
