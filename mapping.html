Every external system is reached through a port with a file-backed adapter, so the operating model was built and tested without access to any of them. This table is the migration plan.

Two of six ports have adapters. That is a deliberate cut: those two are what an asset needs to travel from brief to published, and a slice that runs end to end is worth more than six adapters that have never carried anything.

What the ports buy

The platform decision stops being urgent. It also makes a bake-off possible: run a fixed set of briefs through two adapters behind the same port and compare output quality, cost and integration effort, instead of arguing about it. The pages you are reading were published through a second Channel adapter, swapped in with one constructor argument and no change to the factory core.

What they do not buy

They do not answer whether a given platform can do the drafting well enough. That is measured, not architected. And they do not reduce the compliance work: every adapter that publishes carries the same evidence duty, which is why the ledger is port-independent.
