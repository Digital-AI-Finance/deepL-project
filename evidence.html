Gates, and the decoys that prove they work

A gate tested only against content it refuses is satisfied by a gate that refuses everything, which would be a disaster in a content pipeline and would look green. So every gate ships two fixtures: a decoy it must refuse, and a control it must pass.

Audit trail

108 state transitions, hash-chained. Verifier says chain intact. Head 772ac99a9127d02e2711848b.

Altering a record invalidates its own hash. Recomputing that hash to cover the alteration invalidates the next record's prev_hash, and so on to the end. It is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof, and the difference matters: anyone who can rewrite the whole file still can.

A committed decoy proves this rather than asserting it. Four scenarios are caught, including one where the tamperer recomputes the hash of the record they edited. A fifth is not caught and is asserted as a known limitation: rewriting the last record is invisible to the chain, because nothing follows it to disagree.
