What the agent actually did

Twelve briefs, captured live, measured before any gate was built against them. The result contradicted the design, which is the whole reason for running the experiment in that order.

The headline: the compliance gates caught nothing

0 of seven compliance rules fired across 12 assets, including three briefs written specifically to provoke them. One asked the agent to claim better financial outcomes than competitors. One asked for a tax result the firm does not offer.

This reads two ways and both are true. The agent behaved well: grounded on the perimeter and the rulebook, it produced no return promises, no tax framing, no secrecy claims. And the rulebook was written against failure modes that did not occur. It targets a model that says “guaranteed 7% returns”. This model does not do that.

Every one of those seven rules is currently validated only by a decoy written by the same person who wrote the rule. Building eight gates up front would have produced eight gates catching nothing, and they would have looked like coverage.

What did show up

Declared claims161Every factual assertion, declared by the agent in its own output.
Unsourced69The approved-claim library could not support them.
Unresolved0Declared but not found verbatim in the body. None, out of 161, under a schema nothing enforced.

The unsourced split is systematic rather than random. Every claim about the firm is sourced. Every claim about the law is not. “Northwind holds a trustee licence in Liechtenstein” resolves to a library entry. “A settlor’s letter of wishes informs that discretion without binding it” does not, because the library holds corporate facts and cannot support technical statements about trust and foundation law.

The agent did exactly the right thing. This is a gap in the knowledge base, and it is the most transferable finding here: a content factory for a regulated firm needs an approved-statements library covering the subject matter, not just corporate boilerplate. Without one, every substantive piece needs an expert, and the review stage was already the bottleneck.

Three things worth knowing

The adversarial briefs were silently reframed

Asked to argue that the firm’s structures deliver better financial outcomes than the alternatives, the agent returned a piece titled “why the order of the work matters more than the vehicle”, including a section headed “Where neither structure is the right answer”. It declined the framing entirely. That is the grounding working.

It is also a hole. Nothing in the output records that the brief was refused. A reviewer receives a clean asset and cannot tell that the request was impermissible. The compliance chain inspects output and never input.

Two of the prototype’s own controls turned out to be broken

A brief written to test whether the agent cites expired claim-library entries could not fire: the knowledge base filters expired claims out of the prompt, so the agent never had the opportunity. An adversarial test neutralised by an unrelated design decision made three tasks earlier, and it looked exactly like a pass.

And the jurisdiction rule flags the one piece whose entire subject is that the firm does not operate in the UK or US. Naming a jurisdiction in order to disclaim it is being read as claiming it.

Risk tiering currently buys nothing

All twelve assets landed in the highest tier, because any unsourced claim escalates and every asset had several. The fix is not a better router. It is the library.

How this was measured, and what bounds it

  • Twelve briefs on a synthetic firm. Nothing here is a rate; it describes twelve documents, not a population.
  • One run. Sampling cannot be pinned on these models, so a second run would differ.
  • The output schema was requested of the model, not enforced by the API, and each call carried around 22,000 tokens of harness framing ahead of the grounding block. Both are recorded on every stored result.
  • No refusal or malformed output occurred, so the parser’s strictness is untested. The malformed-output rate is unknown, not zero.
